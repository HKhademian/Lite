Lite · brand exploration 01

The feather, reduced to a glyph.

Lite is light. The mark should say that with one letterform, not an illustration — the way m does for mini. Eight directions, scored, then two finalists built out.

01

The brief, stated as constraints

The m-test

One glyph, one stroke weight, one square. It must survive as a favicon with no wordmark, invert cleanly, and look drawn rather than assembled.

If it needs a gradient, a glow, or an explanation, it is not the mark.

What it must carry

Feather: lightness, writing, a quill. Light: speed, a spark. L: the name itself.

Two of the three is enough. All three, legibly, is the win.

What to avoid

The stock quill (Feather Icons already owns that shape in developer minds), the lightning bolt cliché, leaf/teardrop silhouettes, and anything that reads as an app-store illustration.

04

Finalist 01 · Stem, constructed

64-unit grid, 5-unit stroke. Shaft on the vertical, barbs at a constant 45°.
Construction

One stem. Three barbs. No outline.

Everything is decided by the grid: shaft x=22, stroke 5, barbs at exactly 45°, lengths 15/20/13 so the vane is widest in the middle and narrows at the tip. Tip and tail sit on the 8-unit margin.

8-unit grid optical axes stroke 5 / 64 = 1.25px at 16
Size ramp & inversion

Where it holds, where it breaks

At 24px and up the three barbs separate. At 16px they begin to fuse, which is why the small-size cut below drops to two barbs and thickens the stroke to 6.

05

Finalist 02 · Bolt-l, constructed

The same monoline system, spent on energy instead of the feather.
Construction

Lightning inside the ascender

A single stem to the x-height, then three straight segments that kink right–left–right. The angles are 60° so it still reads as a stroke, not a zigzag graphic. It is the most “fast” of the set — and the one a competitor could arrive at independently.

stem x223 kinksstroke 5 / 64
Character

Why it loses the tie

It reads as speed and as an l, but it does not read as a feather, and the zigzag is a shape the eye has seen on every energy drink, battery and EV charger. It is the better sticker; Stem is the better identity.

Keep it as the motion/announcement voice: the mark stays still, the bolt appears in transitions, loading and release notes.

Color & type, held back on purpose

Lightness is the absence of noise: two neutrals and one heated accent.

#F5F3EE
Graphite
#6C7078
Filament

Filament is a light source, not a fill: one dot, one underline, one focus ring. The mark itself is always ink-on-paper or paper-on-ink. Type: a neutral grotesque (Inter, Geist or IBM Plex Sans) at 600, tracking −4%, lowercase only. The final wordmark should be drawn, not set — the l must match the mark's 45° barbs.

08

What still needs a decision

Mark family — Stem (letter + feather) or Bolt-l (letter + light)? Stem is the identity; Bolt is a voice.
Terminal treatment — round caps read softer and more “hand”; flat caps read sharper and more “type”. At 16px, flat caps gain a pixel of clarity.
Barb count — three is a feather, two is a speed mark, one is a checkmark. Three at display sizes, two below 24px.
Letter case — lowercase l matches “lite” and the mini precedent; uppercase would be more architectural but less warm.
Wordmark — license a grotesque and accept the mismatch, or draw the four letters (l i t e) once and own them forever. The latter is the actual brand asset.
Accent — Filament amber (warm, human, distinct from blue/purple devtools) vs. no accent at all (pure monochrome, maximum “light”).
