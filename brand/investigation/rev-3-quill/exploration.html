Lite · brand exploration 01 · 2026-11-12

The feather, reduced to a glyph.

Lite is light. The mark should say that with one drawn stroke, not an illustration — the way m does for mini. This sheet is the result of testing the obvious feather first, then cutting it down until only what survives at 16 px is left.

Recommendation

The Quill — one shaft, three barbs, no outline.

A shaft and three barbs at a 35° lean. It reads as a feather at 48 px, as motion at 24 px, and still as a deliberate mark at 16 px. The l link is carried by the wordmark, not forced into the icon; that keeps the glyph honest and the lockup typographic — the same division of labour that makes mini's m work.

Mark Quill Type lowercase grotesque, −5% Accent Filament
01

The brief, stated as constraints

The m-test

One glyph, one stroke weight, one square. It must survive as a favicon with no wordmark, invert cleanly, and look drawn rather than assembled.

If it needs a gradient, a glow, or an explanation, it is not the mark.

What it must carry

Feather: lightness, writing, a quill. Light: speed, a spark. L: the name.

Two of the three is enough. All three, legibly, is the win.

What to avoid

The stock quill, the lightning bolt cliché, leaf and teardrop silhouettes, anything that reads as an app-store illustration — and any mark that only works at one size.

02

What the first forty sketches proved

Every failure below was drawn, rendered and looked at, not assumed.
REJECTED
FINDING 01

A solid feather becomes an arrow or a leaf.

Once the barbs are filled in, the silhouette loses the feather read and gains a paper-plane or leaf read. Icon-safe, meaning-wrong.

REJECTED
FINDING 02

Barbs on a vertical stem read as a branch, or a K.

The letter-first version is the most tempting and the weakest: a stem with three ticks is a fern, a wheat ear, or a K — never an l.

REJECTED
FINDING 03

A vane beside a stem reads as D, P or a flag.

Any closed vane attached to a vertical shaft stops being a feather and becomes a letter we did not intend.

REJECTED
FINDING 04

Symmetry kills the lightness.

A feather with barbs on both sides reads as a wheat ear and loses all motion. The mark wants one side only.

REJECTED
FINDING 05

Pure abstraction has no anchor.

A single tapered stroke is beautiful, but it reads as a slash, a comma, or a knife. Nothing says feather or Lite.

SURVIVED
FINDING 06

Diagonal, monoline, one-sided is the only survivor.

It keeps the feather read at every size, keeps the stroke system, and turns the lean into speed. The letter link moves to the wordmark.

04

The Quill, constructed

64-unit box, 4.4-unit stroke, shaft at 35°, barbs at 35° to the shaft.
Geometry

Nothing decorative, nothing accidental.

The shaft is one slightly bowed stroke so the feather bends rather than points. Each barb starts on the shaft and sweeps up and outward at the same 35° the shaft leans from vertical, and the three barbs are evenly spaced. The barbs and the shaft are the same weight; the tip overshoots the top barb by one stroke, which is what makes it read as a quill instead of a wing.

8-unit construction grid shaft and vertical stroke 4.4 / 64 = 1.1 px at 16

Color & type, held back on purpose

Lightness is the absence of noise: two neutrals and one heated accent.

Filament is a light source, never a fill: one dot, one focus ring, one status line. The mark itself is always ink on paper or paper on ink. Type is a neutral grotesque (Inter, Geist, IBM Plex Sans) at 600, tracking −5%, lowercase only. The wordmark should eventually be drawn rather than set — the l is the one place where the feather's angle can echo into the type.

07

What still needs a decision

Mark or wordmark first? — the Quill is the icon; "lite" is the identity. If the wordmark gets drawn, the mark can get quieter still.
One icon or two? — the recommendation uses three barbs above 32 px and two below, as an optical cut. The alternative is one drawing everywhere and a slightly muddier 16 px.
35° or 45°? — 45° aligns with the square and looks engineered; 35° looks like a feather. The current recommendation is 35°.
Does the l need rescuing? — if the name must be legible from the icon alone, direction C (Spark) is the honest choice; the Quill accepts that the wordmark carries the name.
Accent or no accent? — Filament amber makes the mark feel warm and human; pure monochrome is more "light". Default: no accent in the mark, amber only in UI.
Own the sketch? — the quill silhouette is familiar. The next step is to redraw it from scratch as a filled, tapered silhouette so it cannot be mistaken for a stock icon, then test that against this line version.
